Lay the next step

Two things do it. Money, and one adult who stays.

Give

A student who runs out of money rarely leaves school on one dramatic afternoon. First the after-school classes go. Then comes a part-time job. Then they stop bringing up university, because bringing it up hurts. By the time anyone around them notices, the child has already, quietly, narrowed the range of what they allow themselves to want.

The Hope Stair Foundation exists to interrupt that sequence early. There are two ways to do it: money, and one adult who stays.

We were founded on 31 May 2026 in Yangpyeong, Gyeonggi-do, Republic of Korea. The scale is small on purpose. We would rather change a few students all the way through than pass by a thousand.

Where your gift goes

Not overhead. Not campaigns. These things:

  • Tuition and school feesSo that a bank balance is not what decides how a semester turns out.
  • The cost of studyingTextbooks, transport, a desk, a lamp, an internet connection.
  • One semester without a part-time jobFor a student in their final year of high school, this is the difference between really trying and going through the motions.
  • MentoringA trained adult meets the student regularly, for as long as it takes, working through the seven stages of 7 Hope Stairs together.

How to give

You can give through PayPal.

Give via PayPal

paypal.me/hopestair

You do not need a PayPal account — in most countries you can pay by credit or debit card as a guest. There is no minimum and no maximum.

A note on amounts. PayPal takes 4.4% + USD 0.30 on an international payment, and another 3% to convert it into Korean won — roughly 7.4% + 30 cents in total. Because of that fixed 30 cents, the smaller the gift the more of it disappears: about 10% of a US$10 gift, about 8% of a US$30 gift. If you were planning to send several small amounts, one slightly larger gift puts more of it in a student's hands.

Where you can check how it is spent

  • Our annual report is published on this website.
  • All funds are held in a single account in the foundation's name. No personal account sits in between.
  • Each year's budget and financial statements are reviewed by the board, audited, and approved by the general assembly.
  • The foundation is not-for-profit and politically neutral. No income may be used for anything outside its stated purpose.
  • If the foundation dissolves, nothing returns to its founder. Remaining assets go to the state, to a local government, or to another non-profit doing the same work. That is Article 25 of our charter.

Questions we actually get

Can I get a tax receipt?

Not at present. We are an unincorporated association and have not yet been designated a public-interest organization by Korea's National Tax Service. There is no tax benefit for overseas donors in their own countries either. We would rather tell you plainly now than have you find out later.

Who chooses the students?

The board does, as set out in our charter. We look at circumstances and effort, not only grades.

Can I sponsor one particular student?

We do not accept gifts directed at a named individual — this is to protect the students. You may earmark a gift for a purpose, such as tuition, living costs, or the mentoring program, and we will spend it exactly there.

Is this connected to the book Climbing Hope?

Yes. The book's author founded this foundation, and the seven-stage program we run with students is the one described in the book. But buying the book is not a donation, and a donation is not a book purchase. They are separate — please do only whichever one you actually want to do.
